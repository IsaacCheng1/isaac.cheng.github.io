Everything about RL

Reinforcement Learning

It focuses on sequential decision making problems, by observing, taking action, observing, taking action, …

It learns from its own experience. It is more like intelligence.

RL is used in generative models and post-training.

How to model behavior ?

How to represent experience as data ?

  • \(s_t\) - state at time t
  • \(o_t\) - observation at time t
  • \(a_t\) - action/decision taken at time t
  • \(\pi_t\) - trajectory, a sequence of states/observations and actions
    • often referred to as rollout or episode
  • \(r(s,a)\) - reward of \(s,a\)

Markov Property

The state \(s_3\) is independent of its previous state \(s_1\), given state \(s_2\). This helps us to break down the problem in terms of that we do not need to worry about all the dependencies that come before it.

Deep RL

It uses DL to to learn the policy \(\pi_\theta(a \mid s)\).

Goal of RL

  • Maximize \(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}_{\tau \sim p_\theta(\tau)} \left[\sum_{t}^{T} r(s_t,a_t)\right]\)

Stochastic Policy

Policy is not deterministic, i.e. it is stochastic.

How do you know if a policy is good or bad ?

Value function \(V_\pi(s)\): future expected value starting at \(s\) and following \(\pi\)

Imitation Learning

  • given a trajectory (i.e. demonstration) that is collected by experts.
    • demonstration \(D = (s_1, a_1, s_2, a_2, \ldots, s_t)\)
  • the demonstration is sampled from an unknown expert policy \(\pi_{\text{expert}}\)
  • goal: learn the \(\pi_{\text{expert}}\) by imitation.
  • model selection
    • version 0: train a policy \(\pi_\theta\) to predict action \(\hat{a}\) given \(s\), minimizing \(\displaystyle \sum_{(s,a) \in D} \left\|\hat{a} - a\right\|^2\). This is supervised learning.
      • \(\hat{a} = \pi(s)\)
    • version 1: Mixture of Gaussian (GMM, Gaussian Mixture Model).
      • This is a generative model (which learns prob distribution over data).
    • version 2: an auto-regressive model, a sequence model
  • deterministic policy (i.e. output is a certain action) vs. stochastic policy (i.e. output is a distribution of different actions)
